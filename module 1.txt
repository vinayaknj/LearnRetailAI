Module 1: Retail Operating Model Fundamentals
Learning Objective

By the end of this module, learners should be able to:

Explain how a retail business operates end-to-end
Identify major retail systems and their responsibilities
Understand how data flows through retail operations
Describe store hierarchy and ownership structures
Recognize POS dependencies and failure impact
Chapter 1: What is Retail?
Definition

Retail is the business of selling products or services directly to consumers.

Examples:

Walmart
Target
Costco
Kroger
HEB
Amazon Fresh

A retail company performs much more than selling goods.

It must:

Source products
Store inventory
Transport products
Sell to customers
Collect payment
Reconcile finances

Detailed Explanation
1. Supplier

Manufactures products.

Examples:

Coca-Cola
Nestlé
PepsiCo

Outputs:

Product inventory
Product master data
2. Distribution Center (DC)

Central hub for inventory.

Responsibilities:

Receive supplier shipments
Aggregate inventory
Route inventory to stores

KPIs:

Inventory turnover
Order fulfillment rate
3. Warehouse Operations

Tracks:

On-hand inventory
Reserved inventory
Damaged inventory

Systems Used:

WMS (Warehouse Management System)
4. Store Operations

Activities:

Shelf stocking
Product receiving
Cycle counting
Customer service

Store Metrics:

Sales per hour
Stock availability
Shrinkage
5. Checkout

Customer purchases items.

Flow:

Plain Text
Scan Item
↓
Lookup Product
↓
Apply Price
↓
Apply Promotion
↓
Calculate Tax
↓
Payment
↓
Receipt

Primary System:

POS

6. Settlement

After payment:

Plain Text
Customer Card
↓
Payment Processor
↓
Bank
↓
Retailer Account

Settlement may occur later than checkout.

Important distinction:

Sale completed ≠ Money settled

Chapter 6: Online vs Offline Retail
Offline Retail

Flow:

Plain Text

↓

↓
